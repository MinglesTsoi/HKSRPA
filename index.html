The Problem

One Population, Countless Competing Claims

Hong Kong's population of roughly 7.51 million contains dozens of overlapping, often conflicting interest groups — tenants and landlords, employers and employees, developers and residents. Government resource decisions rarely make these trade-offs visible before they are made.

The Solution

HK-SRPA: A Relationship-First Policy Lens

Instead of treating citizens as one undifferentiated public, HK-SRPA models society as a network of relationships — each with its own conflicts, common ground, and power balance — so policymakers can see trade-offs before committing resources.

Multi-Objective, Not Single-Metric

Proof of Concept

Live Demonstration: MiroFish Engine

Our partner George built a working POC on the MiroFish multi-agent simulation engine, hosted at mf.alwayswithyou.cloud, showing how AI agents can role-play distinct stakeholders and react to a policy scenario in real time.

How it works

  • Seed a policy scenario or document
  • Engine casts distinct stakeholder agents
  • Agents debate and react across simulated rounds
  • A structured report captures outcomes and risks

Why it matters for CEPU

  • Fast, low-cost way to pressure-test policy framing
  • Surfaces stakeholder reactions before public consultation
  • Directly maps onto the 12-stakeholder HK-SRPA ontology
  • Extendable into a government-grade decision dashboard

Governance

AI Assists Judgment — It Never Replaces It

Transparent weighting

Value weights are disclosed and open to challenge, not hidden in the model

Minority protection

Vulnerable-group harm cannot be averaged away by majority gains

Preserved uncertainty

Outputs are ranges and scenarios, never false precision

Human sign-off

Final decisions remain with accountable government processes
